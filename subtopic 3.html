Subtopic 3: AI Tools

Link to Cite Pablo Cruz-Gonzalez
Abstract This systematic review examines the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in mental health care, focusing on diagnosis, monitoring, and intervention. Analyzing 85 studies, the review highlights the use of AI methods such as support vector machines and random forests for diagnostic purposes, machine learning for monitoring, and AI chatbots for interventions. The findings suggest that AI tools are effective in detecting, classifying, and predicting mental health conditions, as well as in forecasting treatment responses and monitoring ongoing prognoses. The review emphasizes the need for diverse and robust datasets and calls for enhanced transparency and interpretability of AI models to improve clinical practice
Link to Cite Emma L van der Schyff
Abstract This study explores the development and implementation of Leora, an AI-powered chatbot designed to provide self-led mental health support. Leora engages users in conversations about their mental health, offering assessments, monitoring, and management strategies for minimal-to-mild symptoms of anxiety and depression. The platform provides evidence-based coping strategies and promotes well-being through personalized and discreet support. The study discusses the ethical considerations in AI development for mental health, emphasizing transparency, user privacy, and the importance of AI as a support tool rather than a replacement for professional care.
