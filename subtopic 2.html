Subtopic 2: Mental Health Intervention

Link to Cite Patricia A Arean
Abstract This article examines the potential of mobile technologies, such as smartphone applications and wearable devices, in enhancing mental health assessment and outcome monitoring. Traditional assessment methods often rely on patient self-reports during clinical visits, which may not accurately reflect daily functioning due to recall biases. The authors discuss how mobile technologies can collect real-time data on patients' symptoms, behaviors, and functioning, offering a more nuanced and continuous understanding of mental health status. However, the article also highlights challenges, including concerns about the reliability of consumer-grade apps and sensors, user engagement, and the integration of collected data into clinical decision-making processes. The authors emphasize the need for rigorous research to validate these technologies and to establish guidelines for their effective implementation in mental health care.
Link to Cite Adam C Frank
Abstract This article explores the potential of wearable and mobile technologies in the evaluation and treatment of Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD). It discusses how these devices can monitor symptom burden, estimate diagnosis and risk for relapse, predict treatment response, and deliver digital interventions. The authors highlight that OCD, being a prevalent and disabling psychiatric condition with a chronic and fluctuating course, may uniquely benefit from these technologies.
